# CS-250-Software-Development-Lifecycle
## CS 250 Agile and Scrum Portfolio

Sprint Review and Retrospective

This repository contains my Sprint Review and Retrospective from CS 250 at Southern New Hampshire University. It demonstrates my understanding of Agile and Scrum practices, software development processes, team collaboration, and continuous improvement.

How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

I believe interpreting user needs starts with understanding what the user is trying to accomplish and how the software can meet those expectations. This involves gathering requirements, asking questions, and understanding how the user will interact with the application.

Creating user stories helps translate those needs into smaller, manageable requirements from the user's perspective. User stories also help developers understand a feature's purpose and the expected outcome.

While working on the SNHU Travel project, I learned how important it is to understand requirements before implementing changes. Developing and testing features individually helped me identify problems and make corrections. I also learned that gathering feedback throughout development matters because a feature may work technically but still not meet users' expectations.

How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

My approach to developing programs begins with understanding the requirements, identifying the necessary components, and organizing the work into manageable tasks. I prefer developing and testing features individually rather than waiting until the entire application is complete. This makes it easier to identify problems early and prevent small issues from affecting other parts of the program.

The SNHU Travel project helped me understand the importance of testing, debugging, and maintaining organized code. I learned that even small changes can affect other components of an application.

In future development projects, I would like to incorporate Agile practices such as sprint planning, daily Scrum meetings, backlog refinement, sprint reviews, and retrospectives. These practices help teams communicate, manage priorities, respond to changing requirements, and continuously improve their development processes.

What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

I believe being a good team member in software development means communicating effectively, being accountable for assigned responsibilities, and supporting the team's overall success.

Software development requires collaboration between developers, testers, Product Owners, Scrum Masters, and other stakeholders. Each person contributes to ensuring that the final product meets the customer's expectations.

A good team member should be willing to share knowledge, ask questions, provide constructive feedback, and adapt when requirements change. I also believe team members should focus on shared goals rather than only their individual responsibilities.

One of the most important lessons I learned in CS 250 is that successful Agile teams depend on communication, collaboration, and shared accountability. I plan to carry these practices into future software development projects and professional opportunities.
